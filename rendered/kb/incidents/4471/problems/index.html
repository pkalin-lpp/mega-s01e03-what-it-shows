The #4471 page calls every report one bug: the parser reads dates month first, and a fix is queued. The reports say more. They were merged by subject line (decision 0001), and a subject line cannot tell one bug from another.

#4471 is not one issue. Each problem below has its own ticket. The parser fix queued for #4471 does not fix the others.

parser: day-first dates read month first

The shared parser reads every date month first. A tenant that writes the day first gets day and month swapped: “7 April” comes back as “4 July”. It reaches us through every channel and every tenant on file. This is the fix already queued under #4471.

hint: booking-v3 tells day-first tenants to type the month first

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. The tenant’s people type what the hint tells them, month first, and the form reads it the tenant’s way, day first. Only bookings made through the booking-v3 form are hit. The parser fix does not change it: the hint has to follow the tenant’s locale.

one-day: dates move by one day

No swap here. A date moves by one day, and where the person is can change which day they get: “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.” That points at a date with no time being converted between time zones. Neither the parser fix nor the hint fix touches it.

How each report was placed

  1. One id, one report. The ticket system exports pages that overlap, so a repeated row counts once.
  2. A report the desk read as a one-day move (desk notes) is one-day. Swapping day and month never moves a date by one day.
  3. Every other report goes by its tenant, never by its intake tag: a day-first tenant’s report through booking-v3’s form is hint, any other report from a day-first tenant is parser.
  4. A report from a tenant not on file stays unplaced until someone reads it.

The intake tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser language. The table shows it next to each problem so you can see how often the guess is wrong.

Lessons

  • A subject line says how a report feels, not what caused it. Count by cause, and split a ticket when its reports disagree.
  • The tenant decides the cause. The reporter’s browser does not.
